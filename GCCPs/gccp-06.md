# GCCP-6: Performance-Based Council Remuneration

## Simple Summary

This proposal replaces the current Illuvium Main Council (IMC) remuneration structure with a model directly tied to Illuvium Revenue Distribution (RevDis).

Total monthly council remuneration will be:

**Min($10,000, 1% of Monthly RevDis)**

The total amount will be divided equally among all serving IMC members.

## Proposal

Under GCCP-6:

* **1% of monthly RevDis** will be allocated to IMC remuneration.
* Total IMC remuneration will be capped at **USD $10,000 per month**.
* The total amount will be divided equally among all serving IMC members.
* If no RevDis occurs, council remuneration will be **USD $0**.

### Formula

**Total Council Pay = Min($10,000, 1% of Monthly RevDis)**

Examples:

* $100,000 RevDis → $1,000 total council remuneration
* $500,000 RevDis → $5,000 total council remuneration
* $1,000,000+ RevDis → $10,000 total council remuneration

## Implementation

GCCP-6 will replace the existing remuneration structure established under GCCP-4 beginning with **Epoch 12**.

Payments will continue to be made in ILV based on the USD value calculated under the formula above.

## Rationale

The purpose of GCCP-6 is to align IMC remuneration with the economic performance of the Illuvium ecosystem.

When RevDis is low, council remuneration is low. When RevDis grows, council remuneration grows alongside it, while the **$10,000 monthly cap** limits total compensation.

This removes a fixed council expense and creates a model that is simple, transparent, and easy for the community to verify.

Putting this framework to a **vILV vote before the next council election** also ensures that incoming council members and voters understand the remuneration structure before the new term begins.

The intent is not to undervalue the work of the IMC, but to tie council compensation directly to the health and growth of the ecosystem it serves.

## Governance

Because GCCP-6 directly concerns council remuneration, this proposal is being submitted to **vILV holders for a community vote rather than being decided by the IMC**.

Any future proposal seeking to reinstate a fixed remuneration model or materially change this structure should likewise require a vILV vote.
